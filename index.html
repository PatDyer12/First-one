        How the model works

        Public trade market first, then this season's reality, then what the trade does to your team.

        1

        Market value (primary)

        Every player starts at his FantasyCalc value, which comes from thousands of real trades and is matched to your format (redraft/dynasty, 1QB/superflex). The keeper league uses half redraft, half dynasty. Everything else is a small adjustment on top of this.

        2

        2026 performance

        Actual points per game this season vs. his projection, shrunk hard toward zero because two games is a tiny sample. Capped at ±10%. Usage gets a separate ±4% based on snap share vs. a normal starter at his position (RB 55%, WR 80%, TE 70%).

        3

        Team environment

        Four pieces, capped at ±10% together: offense quality (projected output + points scored), QB situation (the healthy starter's projection; flags when QB1 is out), winning (record; weighs more for RBs because of game script), and teammate health (a key teammate out at another position hurts; at the same position it opens up volume).

        4

        How much this year counts

        Adjustments count fully in redraft, 75% in keeper and 50% in dynasty, because long-term value matters more there. The "This-year factors" slider scales all of it from 0% (pure market) to 150%.

        5

        Roster fit

        Your best legal lineup is rebuilt before and after the trade. The change in starter points per week × weeks left is priced at the market's rate for points, weighted by the "Roster-fit weight" slider (default 25% redraft, 20% keeper, 10% dynasty), and capped at ±25% of the deal. Market value already pays for a player's points, so fit is a tilt, not a second valuation.

        6

        Consolidation

        Each side's best asset counts 100%, then 88%, 76%… (floor 40%). Two quarters ≠ a fifty-cent piece: you can only start so many players, so the side getting the best player gets credit for it.
