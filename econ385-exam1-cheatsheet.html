1. CAUSALITY — does X actually cause Y?

Symbols
Yi(1)Y_i(1)Yi​(1)person iii's outcome if treated
Yi(0)Y_i(0)Yi​(0)person iii's outcome if not treated
DiD_iDi​treatment: 1 = treated, 0 = not treated
τi\tau_iτi​ (“tau”)person iii's treatment effect

Individual effect: τi=Yi(1)−Yi(0)\tau_i = Y_i(1) - Y_i(0)τi​=Yi​(1)−Yi​(0)

ATE (average treatment effect) = average of τi\tau_iτi​ over everyone

ATT (effect on the treated) = average of τi\tau_iτi​ over treated people only

The key equation:

Observed gap === ATT +++ Selection bias

Observed gap =E[Y(1)∣D=1]−E[Y(0)∣D=0]= E[Y(1)\mid D{=}1] - E[Y(0)\mid D{=}0]=E[Y(1)∣D=1]−E[Y(0)∣D=0]

Selection bias =E[Y(0)∣D=1]−E[Y(0)∣D=0]= E[Y(0)\mid D{=}1] - E[Y(0)\mid D{=}0]=E[Y(0)∣D=1]−E[Y(0)∣D=0]

selection bias asks: were the treated people already different, even without treatment?

Steps for a potential-outcomes table

  1. τ\tauτ for each row =Y(1)−Y(0)= Y(1) - Y(0)=Y(1)−Y(0)
  2. ATE = average τ\tauτ of all rows
  3. Observed gap = avg Y(1)Y(1)Y(1) of treated rows − avg Y(0)Y(0)Y(0) of untreated rows
  4. ATT = average τ\tauτ of treated rows
  5. Selection = avg Y(0)Y(0)Y(0) of treated − avg Y(0)Y(0)Y(0) of untreated
  6. Check: ATT + Selection = Observed gap

Big ideas

  • Fundamental problem of causal inference: we only ever see ONE outcome per person (treated or not). The other one — what would have happened — is the counterfactual, and it's missing.
  • Randomized experiment (RCT): chance decides who is treated, so the groups are alike on average → selection bias =0=0=0 → (treatment avg − control avg) estimates the ATE.
  • RCT problems: dropouts/non-compliance, cost, ethics, may not apply elsewhere (external validity).
  • Correlation ≠ causation (and prediction ≠ causation). Other reasons X and Y move together: Y causes X (reverse causality); a third factor drives both (confounder); both just trend over time; pure chance.
  • Deterministic: Y is known exactly from X (°F = 1.8·°C + 32). Stochastic: Y=β0+β1X+uY=\beta_0+\beta_1X+uY=β0​+β1​X+u — there's a random part uuu.

Model: Y=β0+β1X+uY = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X + uY=β0​+β1​X+u.  OLS picks the line that makes the sum of squared residuals as small as possible.

β^1=∑(xi−xˉ)(yi−yˉ)∑(xi−xˉ)2\hat\beta_1 = \dfrac{\sum (x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}{\sum (x_i-\bar x)^2}β^​1​=∑(xi​−xˉ)2∑(xi​−xˉ)(yi​−yˉ​)​     β^0=yˉ−β^1 xˉ\hat\beta_0 = \bar y - \hat\beta_1\,\bar xβ^​0​=yˉ​−β^​1​xˉ

Also β^1=r⋅sYsX\hat\beta_1 = r\cdot\dfrac{s_Y}{s_X}β^​1​=r⋅sX​sY​​ → slope has the same sign as the correlation.

Steps by hand

  1. Find xˉ\bar xxˉ and yˉ\bar yyˉ​.
  2. Make columns: x−xˉx-\bar xx−xˉ,  y−yˉy-\bar yy−yˉ​,  (x−xˉ)2(x-\bar x)^2(x−xˉ)2,  (x−xˉ)(y−yˉ)(x-\bar x)(y-\bar y)(x−xˉ)(y−yˉ​).
  3. Add up the last two columns → plug into β^1\hat\beta_1β^​1​, then β^0\hat\beta_0β^​0​.
  4. Predicted: y^=β^0+β^1x\hat y = \hat\beta_0+\hat\beta_1 xy^​=β^​0​+β^​1​x. Residual: u^=y−y^\hat u = y-\hat yu^=y−y^​.
  5. Check: residuals add to 0, and ∑xu^=0\sum x\hat u = 0∑xu^=0.

Where formulas come from: minimize Q=∑(yi−b0−b1xi)2Q=\sum (y_i-b_0-b_1x_i)^2Q=∑(yi​−b0​−b1​xi​)2 (squared so + and − misses don't cancel). Set derivatives to 0:

∂Q∂b0=−2∑(yi−b0−b1xi)=0\frac{\partial Q}{\partial b_0} = -2\sum(y_i-b_0-b_1x_i)=0∂b0​∂Q​=−2∑(yi​−b0​−b1​xi​)=0 → ∑u^i=0\sum \hat u_i = 0∑u^i​=0

∂Q∂b1=−2∑xi(yi−b0−b1xi)=0\frac{\partial Q}{\partial b_1} = -2\sum x_i(y_i-b_0-b_1x_i)=0∂b1​∂Q​=−2∑xi​(yi​−b0​−b1​xi​)=0 → ∑xiu^i=0\sum x_i\hat u_i = 0∑xi​u^i​=0

Divide the first by nnn: yˉ=β^0+β^1xˉ\bar y = \hat\beta_0 + \hat\beta_1\bar xyˉ​=β^​0​+β^​1​xˉ → the line always passes through (xˉ,yˉ)(\bar x,\bar y)(xˉ,yˉ​).

Changing units: multiply yyy by ccc → slope and intercept both ×c\times c×c. Multiply xxx by ccc → slope ÷c\div c÷c, intercept same. (Dollars → cents: slope ×100\times 100×100.)

How to interpret (fill in the blanks)

Slope: “Each additional [unit of X] is associated with a β^1\hat\beta_1β^​1​ [units of Y] change in Y, on average.”

Intercept: predicted Y when X = 0 (often meaningless if X = 0 is outside the data).

Prediction: plug x into y^=β^0+β^1x\hat y=\hat\beta_0+\hat\beta_1xy^​=β^​0​+β^​1​x.

Not causal because: units with more X may differ in other ways (ability, income) that sit in uuu; no random assignment.

Key assumption: E[u∣X]=0E[u\mid X]=0E[u∣X]=0

the “other factors” average out to zero at every value of X.

If true, the line gives the average Y at each X. You don't need it just to calculate β^\hat\betaβ^​ — only to interpret it. It fails if, e.g., family income affects test scores AND is related to class size.

Residuals always adding to 0 is just math — it does not prove E[u∣X]=0E[u\mid X]=0E[u∣X]=0.
